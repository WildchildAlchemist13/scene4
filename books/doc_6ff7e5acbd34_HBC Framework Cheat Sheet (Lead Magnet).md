THE 5-TYPE CLIFFHANGER ROTATION
1 REVELATION

A secret explodes into the open. The audience knew something was coming — but not this. e.g. Grandma
already knows the engagement is fake.

2 DANGER

An external threat materialises at the worst possible moment, derailing everything. e.g. The licence is revoked
the night before the ceremony.

3 EMOTIONAL

A feeling surfaces that neither character (nor the audience) expected. e.g. He realises he's in love during the
rain scene.

4 ARRIVAL

A disruptive new character walks in and changes every dynamic instantly. e.g. The ex-girlfriend crashes the
Introduction Ceremony.

5 DECISION

A character must make an irreversible choice — right now, on screen, with everything at stake. e.g. Sign the
contract or walk away forever.

3 NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES
The 3-Second Audit
Look at the first 3 seconds of every
episode. Is someone talking about the
weather? Delete it. Start with a slap, a
shout, or a revelation.

The 45-Second Dialogue Test
Read your dialogue aloud. If a
conversation takes longer than 45
seconds to reach the point, cut it down.
Characters in micro-dramas never
agree — every exchange is a sparring
match.

The Paywall Trap
The paywall hits around Episode 6–8.
These episodes must be the absolute
emotional peak of your first arc. If
viewers don't feel urgency here, they
won't pay. Rewrite until it hurts to stop.

THE VELOCITY FRAMEWORK
Pacing Your 80-Episode Arc for Maximum Retention

THE 5 VELOCITY ZONES
ZONE 1

RAPID

Eps 1–10

Breakneck intro. Establish the premise, the fake contract, and the first impossible choice.
Every episode must be viral-ready.

ZONE 2

INCREASING

Eps 11–25

Stakes deepen. Cultural pressure mounts. The characters begin to crack each other's
armour — but only slightly.

ZONE 3

FAST

Eps 26–50

Emotional chaos. High-stakes confrontations, explosive secrets, and the first genuine
moment of real feeling between leads.

ZONE 4

BREAKNECK

Eps 51–70

Maximum chaos. The truth is weaponised. The wedding is cancelled. The audience
cannot breathe.

ZONE 5

RESOLVED

Eps 71–80

Rising-then-resolving. The pursuit, the earned reconciliation, and the final emotional
payoff the audience stayed 80 episodes for.

THE TENTPOLE PLOTTING SYSTEM
You cannot sustain 80 episodes on a single concept. Build your series around 3–4 cultural or dramatic tentpole events — moments
with intrinsic, unstoppable momentum. Once a ceremony is announced, stopping it is catastrophic. That pressure is your plot engine.
